## SECTION 12: Interview Talking Points

**Most Impressive Achievements to Lead With**
- Completing a self-imposed, unsupervised 60-day AI mastery challenge with no academic or employer obligation driving it.
- Maintaining a publicly visible, version-controlled GitHub repository documenting the entire process — turning a personal learning effort into something verifiable and shareable.

**Key Stories to Discuss**
- **The "why I started" story:** What specifically made you decide to run a structured 60-day challenge before your degree even began, instead of waiting for college to teach you. (This is your strongest narrative — lead interviews with it.)
- **The "how I stayed consistent" story:** A specific moment where you didn't feel like continuing the challenge but committed to the daily commit anyway. Concrete detail here will matter more than the general claim of "discipline."
- **The "what I'd do differently" story:** Be ready to name one thing about the challenge or your prompting approach you'd change now — interviewers test self-awareness, not just effort.

**Areas Recruiters Are Likely to Ask About**
- "Have you built anything beyond documenting your learning?" — Be ready to discuss your most recent or in-progress project (see Section 10 roadmap); if you haven't started one yet, be honest and frame it as your immediate next step.
- "What programming languages do you actually know?" — Your resume currently lists CS fundamentals and problem-solving rather than a specific language; expect this gap to come up directly, so have an honest, forward-looking answer ready (e.g., what you're learning now and why).
- "Walk me through your GitHub repo." — Know your own commit history well enough to talk through 2–3 specific days/entries in detail, not just describe it in the abstract.
- "Why prompt engineering / AI specifically, and not general software engineering?" — Have a genuine, specific answer ready; recruiters can tell when this is a borrowed talking point versus an actual interest.
- "What will you do differently once formal coursework starts in August?" — Show that you see the degree as additive to your self-driven work, not a reason you'll stop it.
